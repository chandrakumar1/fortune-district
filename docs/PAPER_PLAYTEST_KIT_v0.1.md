PART 2 — Rules card (read this to your players, ~90 seconds)

You're competing to be the richest operator in the district after 12 rounds.
Nobody gets knocked out. Richest at the end wins.

You start with ₵1,200. Every time you pass or land on the Hub, you get ₵150.

On your turn I roll two dice. You add them up and move exactly that many spaces
forward. [revised]

Land on an unowned property, you can buy it. If you say no, it goes to auction —
everyone bids at once, minimum half price, highest bid wins.

Land on someone else's property, you pay them. But no single payment can take more
than a quarter of your cash. Anything above that becomes debt, and debt grows 10%
each round. Debt doesn't knock you out, it just counts against you at the end.

Own all three properties of a category and your income from them doubles.
You can buy one upgrade per turn on anything you own.

Every round, whoever is last place gets a Leverage token. Spend one to force a
property to auction, take ₵200 interest-free, or block the Pulse penalty for a round.

The city economy shifts at rounds 4, 7 and 10 — one category earns 50% more, another
earns 40% less. You always see the next shift one round before it happens.

PART 3 — Running it

You are the banker and rules-keeper. Do not play. You need your attention on their faces, not your own cash. Three friends is enough; the design supports 2–4.

Use a phone timer at 30 seconds per decision (not 20 — paper arithmetic is slow and you're testing the decision, not the maths).

Roll both dice openly and say both numbers and the total out loud before moving
the piece. [revised]

Do not explain your design. Don't say "the idea is that…" or defend a rule when someone complains. Say "noted" and write it down. The complaint is the data.

Don't fix anything mid-game. Play the broken version to the end.

Time the whole match, start to finish.

PART 5 — Observation sheet

Fill this in within ten minutes of finishing, while it's fresh. One or two lines each.

1. Did anyone get bored? When exactly — which round, and what were they doing?

2. Was the winner obvious before round 10? Ask each player directly: "at what point
did you know who'd win?" Write their round numbers down.

3. Did the last-place player feel they had meaningful moves? Ask them straight out:
"did you feel like you could still do something, or were you just going through the
motions?"

4. [retired — the two-dice choice was removed in the 14 Sep 2026 revision; movement
is now the sum of both dice. Leave this question blank.]

5. Did the property/upgrade/economy loop feel fun or confusing? Note every rule you
had to explain twice, and every point where someone asked "wait, why?"

Also worth capturing:

The first thing each player said unprompted after the game ended.

Anything anyone tried to do that the rules didn't cover.

Whether they wanted to play a second time without being asked.

That last one is the single most honest signal you will get all week.

What I'll be looking for in your report

The three failure modes I'd bet on, so you know what to watch:

Rent dodging. [revised — with sum movement players can no longer steer around developed tiles; watch instead whether debt ever triggers and whether payments feel trivial.]

Sets never complete. Categories are spread 8 tiles apart on a 24-tile board with ~2 laps per game [revised: with sum movement the average move is 7, so roughly 3.5 laps per game]. Watch whether anyone finishes one at all.

Dead rounds 11–12. If the last two rounds feel like formalities, the endgame swing isn't working and the round-10 Pulse isn't doing its job.

If all three show up, the fix is structural and I'd rather find it now on paper than
after you've written the rules engine.
